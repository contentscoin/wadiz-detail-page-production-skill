# Service / Intangible Offer Detail Page Workflow

Use this reference when the Wadiz-style detail-page subject is not a physical product: service, membership, course, consulting, B2B package, event, hospitality, tour, subscription, SaaS onboarding, professional profile, clinic/program, or any offer where the buyer cannot judge value from a product photo alone.

## Core distinction

A physical product page proves value through object detail: shape, material, package, usage, spec, before/after, reviews, delivery.

A service/intangible page proves value through transformation and trust: buyer problem, promised outcome boundary, service journey, people, process, proof, operating conditions, risk removal, and consultation CTA.

Do not force a service into product-photo logic. If there is no product photo, switch from `product detail analysis` to `service evidence analysis`.

## Service fact map

Collect or mark `confirmation_needed`:

- service / program / membership name
- target buyer and purchase situation
- buyer pain or job-to-be-done
- desired outcome, with claim boundaries
- included deliverables and excluded items
- process / curriculum / operation flow
- duration, frequency, locations, channels, capacity, schedule
- price, VAT, options, deposit/refund/cancel rules
- staff / experts / instructors / partners and verified bios
- proof assets: case studies, portfolio, reviews, measurable records, before/after, event photos, facility photos, credentials
- trust assets: official site, policy docs, contracts, notices, FAQ, caution/legal text
- brand assets: logo, palette, approved photos/videos
- regulated or sensitive claims to avoid

For professional services, verified people/process/proof are the equivalent of product specs. Missing bios, process details, and proof assets are production blockers.

## Service story spine

Default 15-cut structure for high-consideration services:

1. Situation hook — name the buyer problem or opportunity
2. Why ordinary alternatives fail — current workaround, hidden cost, missed opportunity
3. Service reveal — what this service/membership/program is
4. Outcome boundary — what the buyer can expect and what is not guaranteed
5. Buyer fit — who it is for / not for
6. Service journey — step-by-step process, timeline, or operating flow
7. People / expert proof — real verified profiles, roles, credentials
8. Scene proof — photos/video/GIF/storyboard of service delivery or environment
9. Deliverables — what the buyer receives, with concrete artifacts or touchpoints
10. Use cases — 3–5 scenarios mapped to buyer goals
11. Differentiation — factual comparison with alternatives, no unsupported superiority
12. Offer / pricing — price, package, options, VAT, inclusions/exclusions
13. Objection handling — schedule, location, capacity, customization, risk, support
14. Caution / policy — cancellation, refund, tax/legal/compliance boundaries
15. Consultation CTA — next action, qualification questions, contact path

Use 12 cuts for simpler services by merging 2+4, 7+8, and 13+14.

## Visual production rules

Services often fail because the agent makes generic mood images. Every cut must have an evidence-bearing visual role:

- People proof: real staff/expert portraits or verified profile cards
- Process proof: timeline, checklist, operating flow, meeting/lesson/session sequence
- Environment proof: place, equipment, facility, event, service scene
- Deliverable proof: report, dashboard, curriculum, voucher, membership card, schedule, before/after document
- Scenario proof: buyer use cases shown as realistic scenes
- Policy proof: clear information card for conditions and cautions

If real photos are missing, generate only concept scenes and label publication blocked. Do not present stock-looking scenes as proof.

## Copy pattern

Service copy should be specific, not slogan-heavy:

- Replace object-feature bullets with `problem → intervention → buyer result → evidence`.
- Use verbs that describe delivery: diagnose, design, assign, coordinate, operate, report, follow up.
- State boundaries: `상황에 따라`, `협의 후`, `내부 기준 확인 필요`, `전문가 검토 권장`.
- Avoid guaranteed outcomes, legal/medical/financial certainty, or fake testimonials.
- Move internal statuses (`pack_not_verified`, `asset_gate`) to QA docs, never customer-facing cuts.

## Asset gate for service pages

`asset_gate_ready` requires enough of:

- official source or fact sheet for service terms
- verified team/expert bios if named people appear
- approved logo/brand assets
- real service-scene photos/video or explicitly approved generated-scene route
- proof assets or portfolio/case records
- legal/policy/caution copy for sensitive claims

If any named expert, proof claim, price, or policy is missing, the page can be a plan/concept only.

## QA checks

Before delivery, verify:

- Does the page explain what the service actually does by cuts 03–06?
- Does every proof-looking element have a source or is it labeled concept?
- Are named people backed by verified bios?
- Are outcomes bounded and non-guaranteed?
- Are inclusions/exclusions, price, VAT, schedule, cancellation/refund/caution handled?
- Does it still feel like a Wadiz detail page rather than a company homepage?
- Are visuals varied across situation, process, people, proof, offer, FAQ, and CTA?
